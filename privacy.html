Privacy Policy

Vacuum Rush, version 2.0 of this policy. Effective 2026-11-23.

Short version. Vacuum Rush is free and shows ads, served by Google AdMob. The game has no accounts of its own, no analytics service and no crash-reporting service, and we never receive your progress or personal data. If you are old enough for ads (16 in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, 13 elsewhere), Google's ads software runs on your device and collects the data described in section 3, including your advertising ID. You choose whether ads are personalised, and you can change that at any time in Settings. If you are under that age, you get no ads and Google's ads software is never started. On iOS, if you are signed in to Apple Game Center, your scores and achievements go to Apple for the leaderboards.

2. What the game does on your device

  • Your progress and settings (levels, trophies, unlocked items, chosen options) are saved in files on your own phone. They are never uploaded to us. If you uninstall the game or clear its data they are deleted, unless your phone's own backup (for example Google backup or iCloud) keeps a copy, which Google or Apple controls.
  • Basic usage statistics and a crash log are written to a file on your phone for debugging on that device. They are not uploaded and we cannot read them. The game has no analytics or crash-reporting service. (The "Analytics" and "Crash reports" choices in the privacy notice are stored for a possible future version; today nothing is sent.)
  • Your privacy choices and age answer are saved on your phone: which options you chose in our privacy notice, when, which version of this policy it referred to, whether your device looked like it is in the EEA/UK/Switzerland or elsewhere, and the result of the age screen (section 4: only "old enough for ads" or "not", never your birth year). They are not uploaded to us.
  • Delete my data (Settings) erases the save file, the statistics, the crash log, your privacy choices and your age answer from your phone. The privacy notice then appears again at once and the age screen at the next launch; until you answer the age screen you get no ads. It does not change what Google already holds or what Google's consent tool has stored (use Ad privacy options for that, section 3; see also section 10).
  • Apple Game Center (iOS only, optional): on iPhone and iPad the game uses Apple Game Center if you are signed in to it on your device. Then the game sends your best scores and the achievements you unlock to Apple, so they appear in the leaderboards and your Game Center profile, and it shows your Game Center player name inside the game. We never receive this data, and the player name is not uploaded by us anywhere. Apple handles it under Apple's privacy policy, and you control it in your device's Game Center settings. If you are not signed in to Game Center, nothing is sent. Game Center does not exist on Android, so none of this applies there.
  • Real-money purchases are switched off in this version, so no payment data is involved.

3. Ads (Google AdMob)

Which ads. The game shows two kinds of ads: rewarded ads, which you choose to watch in exchange for a bonus (for example doubling coins, a revive or a free coin gift), and short full-screen ads between rounds, which appear only after some play time and never in the first session. There are no banner ads, no ads while you play and no ads when the game starts.

Who provides them. One advertising provider: Google AdMob, through the Google Mobile Ads SDK (Google's Unity plugin, version 11.7.0). There is no mediation and no other ad network. The ad software does not start when you open the game. Google's consent tool runs only after the age screen says you are old enough and you have seen our own notice, and the ad software itself starts only after the age screen says you are old enough (section 4), after you have seen our own privacy notice, and after Google's consent tool (the Google User Messaging Platform, "UMP") reports that ads may be requested.

What the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects. According to Google's published disclosure for the SDK (Google Mobile Ads Unity plugin, data disclosure), it collects automatically, and Google uses for showing and measuring ads, analytics and fraud prevention and security:

  • device and account identifiers: your advertising ID (on Android the Google advertising ID, which you can reset or delete in your Android settings; on iOS Apple's advertising identifier, which you control under Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking), the app set ID, and other identifiers tied to a signed-in account, where applicable;
  • your IP address, from which Google can estimate your general location (the game requests no precise location and holds no location permission);
  • ad interactions: app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostics: performance data of the app and the ad software, such as launch time, hang rate and energy use.

Google states that everything the SDK collects is encrypted in transit (TLS). We do not receive this data, apart from aggregated ad reports in our AdMob account. We do not add your name, email address or game progress to ad requests, and we do not sell data. The game asks Google to serve only ads rated suitable for teens or younger. Google's own explanation of what it does with data from apps that use its services is at How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services, and its general policy at policies.google.com/privacy.

Consent order. (1) The age screen. (2) Our own privacy notice, where you can accept all, reject all or manage choices, including Personalised ads. (3) Where Google requires it (for example in the EEA, UK and Switzerland), Google's own consent form, which is shown at launch when needed. The game requests ads only if Google's consent tool says ads may be requested. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland we rely on your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)) for the ad software's access to and storage of information on your device and for personalised ads. Elsewhere the notice is shorter and the choices are on by default, and you can still change them at any time.

Personalised and non-personalised ads. Personalised ads use your advertising ID to pick ads that may be more relevant to you. If you turn Personalised ads off in our notice, or (on iOS) refuse Apple's tracking request, you still see ads, but the game sends Google the non-personalised flag. A choice you make in Google's own form is applied by Google's software. Per Google's documentation, non-personalised ads do not use your advertising ID to choose the ad, although Google may still process some data for frequency capping, ad measurement and fraud prevention.

iOS tracking prompt. On iOS the game asks for Apple's "App Tracking Transparency" permission only if all of this holds: you are old enough for ads, you left personalised ads on in our notice, you have not yet answered Apple's prompt, and you are on the main menu (never during a round). If you refuse, your ads are non-personalised. Android has no such prompt; there the advertising ID is controlled in your Android settings.

How to change or withdraw your choice.

  • Settings → Ad privacy options opens Google's privacy-options form, where you can change or withdraw your consent, wherever Google says one is required (such as the EEA, UK and Switzerland). Where none is required, the game tells you so. When you withdraw or change your choice, the game discards the ads it had already loaded and requests new ones under the new setting.
  • Settings → Privacy choices reopens our own notice, where you can switch Personalised ads on or off.
  • In your Android settings you can reset or delete your advertising ID and turn off ads personalisation (usually under Settings → Privacy or Google → Ads). On iOS use Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

4. Age screen and players under the age limit

When ads are available in your build, the game first asks for your birth year (nothing else; no year is shown until you tap, and no hint of the limit). It keeps only the result, "old enough for ads" or "not", on your phone. It never stores or uploads your birth year. The limit is 16 in the EEA, UK and Switzerland and 13 elsewhere. The game decides which applies from your device's region, language and time zone, and treats you as being in the EEA, UK or Switzerland when it cannot tell. It also assumes your birthday has not happened yet this year, so a player close to the limit is treated as under it.

If you are under the limit, or you have not answered, no ads are shown and Google's ad software is never started: no consent form from Google, no initialisation, no ad request, no advertising ID read by the game, and on iOS no tracking prompt. The game stays fully playable and the menus leave out the ad buttons. Our notice states that no data is used for ads. The age screen is a declaration by you and cannot prove your age; it is a neutral safeguard, not a verification. If you enter a wrong year, "Delete my data" in Settings lets you answer again (the age screen returns at the next launch).

5. What the game does not do

  • It does not ask you to create an account with us (on iOS it only uses your existing Game Center sign-in, if any), and it does not ask for or collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, microphone, camera, location or payment details.
  • It contains no third-party analytics, attribution or crash-reporting software. The only third-party software that contacts the network on your device for its own purposes is Google's ads software described in section 3 (and Apple Game Center on iOS).
  • It does not run Google's ad software for players under the age limit (section 4).
  • It does not track you across other companies' apps or websites for ourselves. Advertising IDs are handled by Google as described above, subject to your choices.

11. Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for access to, correction or erasure of your data, restriction of its processing, portability, and you can object to processing based on legitimate interests; where processing is based on consent you can withdraw it at any time (section 3), without affecting what happened before. Because the game stores nothing about you on our side, the data we could hold about you is limited to the emails you send us. For ad data held by Google, use Google's privacy tools above or contact Google; for Game Center data held by Apple, use your Apple account's privacy controls. Write to shopglowave@gmail.com; we answer within one month. You may also complain to the Portuguese data protection authority, the Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados (CNPD), or to the authority of your own country.

12. Children

Vacuum Rush is not directed at children under 13 and is not part of Google Play's Designed for Families programme. If you believe a child has sent us personal data (for example by email), write to us and we will delete it.
